Terrarium

A sketch for a norns instrument where the patch is a small landscape behind glass. Four slots each draw one part of the scene: the brain is the weather, the voice is the ground, the color is the light and water, the space is the air. Swap a module and that part of the scene changes. Turn a macro and the scene moves with the sound. The screen shows no numbers.

E1 picks a part. Everything else dims for a moment so you can see which part you're steering. E2 and E3 are that part's two macros. K2 swaps its module; hold K2 and turn E2 to pick the module's variant: the Plaits engine (each grows its own flower), the DX bank, the Rings model, the Clouds mode. K3 is a gesture: a new phrase on the brain, a manual strike on the voice and color, freeze on the air. Hold K1 to peek at the parts and reach the settings: while it's held, E2 picks a setting and E3 changes it.

Every note is a place. Where an event lands across the screen sets its pitch within the key and scale from the settings, so a melody is something you can see. The brain keeps its phrase in a Turing Machine-style register: each stored note leaves a mark on the ground, and change sets how often notes get replaced, dropped to a rest, or added back. Turn change all the way down and the phrase repeats forever.

Seasons lets the key turn on its own. At the end of a phrase it moves to a close neighbor that shares all but one or two notes, like dorian to minor or C to G. Shared notes stay where they are, so the phrase carries on and only a note or two shifts. The key you set is home: the seasons drift away and keep finding their way back. Slow, steady and quick turn every 8, 4 or 2 phrases.

The sounds here are rough Web Audio stand-ins. On norns each module would be the real mi-UGen (Plaits, Rings, Ripples, Clouds, Beads, MiVerb) or any nb voice, and the drawing code ports almost line for line to the norns screen API.
